# GRIMMPOP — Tales That Bite Back
**Release 1.0 · single-file mobile web game · portrait · one-thumb controls**

A dark-fairytale bubble-trapping arcade platformer. You are a cursed child of the old tales. Blow bubbles, trap the fiends of the Grimm woods, pop them for treasure, chain the pops together, and survive 1,000 tales, an endless tower, a roguelite curse run, and a daily curse.

This document explains **everything about the game**: how it plays, every system, every number, every enemy, every boss, every hero, every upgrade, and how the levels are made. It is written so you can design, balance, market, or extend the game without reading the code.

## 2. The pitch in 30 seconds

You play a tiny cursed hero on a single screen of stone platforms. Fiends wander the screen. You can't hurt them directly, but your bubbles are made of trapped soul-light: hit a fiend and it's **sealed in a bubble** that floats upward. Touch the bubble to **pop** it, and the fiend bursts into treasure. Pop bubbles that are **close together** and they **chain**, each pop multiplying the score. Clear the screen before the dark swallows it. Beat a **boss** every tenth tale. Find **the Hoard** hidden behind a big chain. Then do it again, forever.

Each level is built to last **roughly 30 to 60 seconds**, so it fits quick phone sessions, but the game never runs out of levels.

## 3. Controls

### Touch (designed for one thumb)
| Action | How |
|---|---|
| **Move** | Touch anywhere and **drag left or right**. A small dead-zone (12 px) prevents jitter. The drag "anchor" trails your thumb (34 px), so reversing direction is instant. |
| **Jump** | **Flick your thumb up** (26 px upward) while dragging, **or quick-tap** (under about a quarter second with almost no movement). A **second finger tap** also jumps. |
| **Shoot** | **Automatic** (one-thumb mode). The hero fires a bubble on its own at any fiend in range. |
| **Pause** | Tap the **II** button (top left). The game also auto-pauses if you switch apps or tabs. |

### Two-thumb mode (optional)
Switch schemes with the **CONTROLS** button on the main menu or the pause screen. The choice is saved.
| Action | How |
|---|---|
| **Move** | **Left thumb:** touch anywhere on the left half. A floating joystick appears under your thumb. Push left or right to run. |
| **Jump** | Push the stick **up**, or tap the small yellow **▲** button (right side, lower). |
| **Fire** | **Right thumb:** touch and **hold** anywhere on the right half (the magenta **FIRE** circle marks the spot). Bubbles fly the way you face, at the normal rate of about one per 0.4 s. |
In this mode **auto-fire is off**: you aim by facing and choose when to shoot. The default one-thumb mode is unchanged.

### Keyboard (desktop)
| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| ← → or A D | Move |
| ↑, W or Space | Jump |
| Z, X or Shift | Fire a bubble manually (the auto-fire still works as well) |
| P or Esc | Pause |

### Movement feel
- Jump has **coyote time** (you can still jump about 0.09 s after walking off a ledge) and a **jump buffer** (a jump pressed up to 0.13 s early still fires on landing). These make the jump forgiving on touch screens.
- Platforms are **one-way**: you pass up through them from below and land on top.
- The side walls are solid. The **top** of the screen is a ceiling you can't pass.
- **Wrap-around:** if the floor has a gap, you (and enemies, and treasure) fall through the bottom and **reappear at the top** through the matching gap.

## 4. Core gameplay loop

1. **Arrive.** A banner shows the realm name (or the boss's name). You get about 1.2 seconds of "The Tale Begins" with brief protection.
2. **Hunt.** Move and jump around the platforms. Your hero turns and fires a bubble whenever a fiend is roughly level with you and within about 4.7 tiles.
3. **Trap.** A bubble that touches a fiend seals it. The fiend floats up, drifting gently. It stays trapped for **6 seconds** (more with certain heroes and boons). The bubble blinks red for the last 1.6 seconds, and then the fiend **breaks free, furious** (red, faster).
4. **Pop.** Touch a fiend-bubble and it bursts into treasure (fruit, apples, plums and so on). Chain nearby bubbles for multipliers.
5. **Collect.** Treasure bounces on the platforms for 10 seconds. After the last fiend is gone, remaining treasure is pulled to you automatically.
6. **Survive the clock.** See [section 10](#10-time-pressure-the-dark-comes).
7. **Clear.** Earn up to three stars and coins, maybe find the Hoard, then move to the next tale.

You lose a life by touching a free fiend, a boss, a spat projectile, or the hunting ghost. You get **2.2 seconds** of flashing invulnerability after a hit, and you respawn at the start spot (bottom left).

## 5. The four game modes

The game is a merge of four classic structures, each a full mode from the main menu.

### 5.1 THE TALES (Story Realms)
- **1,000 levels** in **100 realms** of 10 levels each. The 10th level of every realm is a **boss**.
- A scrollable realm map shows each realm with its colors, name, and star total (out of 30). Levels unlock **in order**; you can replay any unlocked level to improve its stars.
- **Lives:** 3 per level attempt (4 with the hero *Widow*). Lives reset every time you start a level.
- After clearing a level you see the result card: stars, score, coins, best chain, with **Next Tale / Replay / Realm Map**.
- Level 1000 is the end of the map.
- The story seed is fixed, so **every player sees the same levels**, and Level 37 is always Level 37.

### 5.2 THE ENDLESS TOWER
- An infinite climb. Floor after floor, with **no end**.
- **Same tower for everyone** (fixed seed), so Floor 40 is identical on every device.
- **A boss every 25 floors.** Beating a boss grants **+1 life**.
- **Lives carry across floors:** 3 to start (4 with Widow). Run out and the run ends.
- Scenery and colors change every 10 floors; difficulty keeps rising.
- Your **deepest floor** is saved.
- Floors flow automatically, with no menus between them, so it is the fastest "one more floor" mode.

### 5.3 CURSED RUN (Roguelite)
- Every run uses a **random seed**: a new tower each time.
- After **every floor**, choose **one of three random boons** (see [section 14](#14-boons-roguelite-upgrades)). They stack, so a good combination can snowball.
- **A boss every 10 floors**, each of which also grants +1 life.
- Lives carry across floors (3, or 4 with Widow). Run out and the run ends.
- Your **deepest floor** is saved. Coins earned carry over to buy heroes.

### 5.4 DAILY CURSE
- A short challenge **seeded by today's date**: everyone gets the same challenge on the same day.
- **Three floors**: two regular floors and a **boss** on floor 3 (a lighter "mini" boss with 9 hit-points).
- Each day carries a **modifier** (see [section 15](#15-daily-curse-modifiers)) that changes the rules, such as low gravity or a single life.
- Lives: 3 normally, **1** on Glass Heart day. (Widow's bonus life does not apply to the Daily.)
- It tracks your **best score today** and your **daily streak** (consecutive days played). Finishing awards a bonus of 40 coins.
- You can retry as often as you like; only your best score is kept.

There are **ten distinct realms**, each with its own palette, and then the cycle repeats with a slight color shift and a number suffix ("Briar Hollow 2") up to realm 100. The realm decides: the color of the sky, platforms, walls and glowing eyes; the **boss** you face at the end; and the quality of the **treasure** (see [section 9](#9-bubbles-chains-and-scoring)).

| # | Realm | Hue / mood | Boss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Briar Hollow** | blood-red dusk | The Thorn Queen |
| 2 | **The Gingerbread Gallows** | sickly green | The Gingerbread Witch |
| 3 | **Wolf's Hour Wood** | deep violet | The Wolf in Grandmother |
| 4 | **Cinder Crypt** | ember orange | The Crooked Piper |
| 5 | **The Drowned Mere** | cold teal | The Drowned Bride |
| 6 | **Rotten Orchard** | rot-yellow green | The Scarecrow King |
| 7 | **Bone Bridge** | plum magenta | The Bridge Troll |

Every screen has the same dressing: a pale **moon**, **dead trees** silhouetted against the sky, drifting **fog bands**, and pairs of **glowing eyes** watching from the dark. Platform stones are cracked, with scattered bone fragments and the occasional glowing thorn.

## 7. Enemies (the fiends)

There are **five regular fiends** plus the bosses. New types are introduced as the "difficulty level" rises (see [section 17](#17-difficulty-curve)). All fiends have glowing eyes and jagged teeth, and **turn blood-red when angry**.

All fiends are the same size (16×16 pixels), take one bubble to trap, and drop treasure when popped.

| Fiend | First appears at difficulty | Look | Base speed (px/s) | Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Grubbling** | 1 (always) | olive-green goblin | 38 | Walks back and forth. Turns at walls, and usually turns (about half the time) at platform edges, otherwise steps off. If you are above it and near, sometimes jumps toward you. |
| **Toad** | 3 | blue-green with big legs | 48 | A **hopper**: jumps frequently (about 3 of 4 chances on each jump check), so it moves in arcs and climbs platforms fast. |
| **Wolf** | 8 | grey with ears | 34 | A **charger**: when you are on the same level, in front of it, and within 7 tiles, it **dashes at 2.7× speed**. Walks normally otherwise. |
| **Imp** | 14 | purple with horns | 32 | A **spitter**: every 2.2 to 3.8 seconds, if you are within about 2.5 tiles vertically, it **spits a pink bolt** horizontally toward you (speed 110). Bolts vanish on walls. |
| **Wraith** | 22 | pale tattered ghost | 30 | A **flier**: ignores platforms and walls, **chasing you through the air** with a wobble. It cannot be avoided by hiding behind ledges, so trap it fast. |

How types are chosen: each fiend slot has a 50% chance to be a random **newer** type (anything after Grubbling that is unlocked), otherwise a uniform random pick from everything unlocked. So later levels are a **mix** of everything, not only the newest fiend.

**Speed scaling:** every fiend's speed is multiplied by `1 + min(0.8, difficulty × 0.004)`, so fiends get up to **80% faster**, reaching the cap at difficulty 200. Angry fiends move **1.6× faster** on top of that. Daily modifiers may add more.

**Spawn:** fiends appear on platforms in the chosen layout with a short fade-in (about half a second) during which they can't hurt you. They are never placed in the left part of the floor near your spawn point, and are spread so no two share nearly the same spot.

**Number of fiends** per level = `min(9, 2 + floor(√difficulty × 0.9))` plus any modifier extras:

| Difficulty | Fiends |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2 |
| 2–4 | 3 |
| 5–11 | 4 |
| 12–19 | 5 |
| 20–30 | 6 |
| 31–44 | 7 |
| 45–60 | 8 |
| 61 and up | 9 (the maximum) |

## 8. Bosses (the beasts)

A boss appears as the **10th level of every realm** (Story), **every 25th floor** (Endless Tower), **every 10th floor** (Cursed Run), and **floor 3** of the Daily Curse. Boss levels use a special **arena** (see below), show the **boss's name** on the intro banner, and have **no Dark Comes timer**: you can take your time, but the boss never stops pressuring you.

### 8.1 How every boss fights (shared behavior in 1.0)

> **Important honesty note:** in version 1.0 all ten bosses share the **same attack pattern**; they differ in **name, color, lore, and hit-points** (which scale with the realm). Unique, lore-driven fights per boss are the top item for version 1.1 (see section 22 and the redesign notes in 8.4).

- **Body:** 44×44 pixels (2.75× a regular fiend). Drawn with **horns**, a **third glowing eye**, jagged teeth, and the realm's accent color as its skin. A **magenta health bar** floats above it.
- **Movement:** patrols left and right at about 36 px/s (scaled by difficulty), turning at walls and sometimes at edges.
- **Leap:** every **2.6 seconds** it jumps (a bigger jump than normal) **toward you**, which also lets it climb between platforms and drop through gaps.
- **Triple shot:** every **3.2 seconds** it fires **three pink bolts** in a fan aimed at you (speed 95, ±20° spread). When it drops to **half health or less**, it fires every **2.0 seconds**, which is the "enraged" phase.
- **Summons:** about every **9 seconds** it spawns a **Grubbling** minion, but only if fewer than 3 minions are alive.
- **Contact:** touching the boss costs a life (the contact box is slightly forgiving).
- **Taking damage:** a bubble that hits the boss does **1 damage**, makes it flash, and the bubble is used up. Bosses are **never trapped**; they must be whittled down. Each hit is worth 100 points.
- **Defeat:** the boss explodes in gold and magenta sparks with a screen shake and a long rising sound. It drops **3,000 + 400 × realm** points' worth of bonus score and **14 pieces of high-value treasure**. Any **minions** left on screen are instantly converted into treasure. **A Hoard door is guaranteed** to appear after every boss.

### 8.2 The arena
Boss levels use a fixed layout: a **full solid floor** (no wrap-around), and three platforms above it:
- Row 17: a wide ledge on each side with a **6-tile gap** in the middle
- Row 13: a long central platform with short gaps at each end
- Row 9: again two side ledges with a wide central gap

The boss starts on the upper left. The gaps let it fall through to chase you, and let you drop down to escape.

### 8.3 Boss hit-points
Boss HP = `min(30, 8 + floor(realm × 1.2))`, where `realm` counts from 0.

| Realm | Boss | HP |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Thorn Queen | 8 |
| 2 | The Gingerbread Witch | 9 |
| 3 | The Wolf in Grandmother | 10 |
| 4 | The Crooked Piper | 11 |
| 5 | The Drowned Bride | 12 |
| 6 | The Scarecrow King | 14 |
| 7 | The Bridge Troll | 15 |
| 8 | The Apple Queen | 16 |
| 9 | Old Bluebeard | 17 |
| 10 | The Mirror Witch | 18 |

HP keeps climbing with every realm cycle and **caps at 30** (reached at realm 20 and beyond). The same ten names repeat in later cycles but in the shifted realm colors, and tougher. The **Daily Curse** boss is always a **9 HP** mini-boss, named by the day's realm. In the **Endless Tower**, the bosses at floors 25, 50, 75, 100 are the realm-3, realm-5, realm-8 and realm-10 bosses, with 10, 12, 16 and 18 HP; HP then keeps rising until the cap of 30 at floor 200.

Because auto-fire throws one bubble roughly every 0.4 seconds, a boss fight typically takes **about 20 to 50 seconds** depending on HP and how well you dodge.

### 8.4 The ten beasts (lore bible and planned unique fights)
These are the intended personalities and the **planned signature mechanic** for each boss in 1.1. **(In 1.0 only the name, color and HP differ in play.)** Every boss would also gain phase changes at 66% and 33% health and an enraged final phase.

1. **The Thorn Queen** — *Briar Hollow.* A sovereign grown into her own hedge, crowned in living thorns. Every rose that blooms in her woods is a promise she has collected on. **Planned fight:** she is rooted in the center; thorns telegraph then erupt along platform stretches; in phase 2 roses bloom and must be popped or they heal her.
2. **The Gingerbread Witch** — *The Gingerbread Gallows.* She has been baking children into her walls for centuries, and the candy gallows outside is only for show. Her oven never goes cold. **Planned fight:** floor tiles heat and burn in a moving pattern; candy-glass platforms crack when stood on; she is only vulnerable when her oven door swings open.
3. **The Wolf in Grandmother** — *Wolf's Hour Wood.* A wolf wearing a grandmother's nightcap and a grandmother's smile, a little too wide. **Planned fight:** she starts as an invulnerable sleeping granny, then lunges across the screen; the wolf is only hittable while stunned after a missed lunge.
4. **The Crooked Piper** — *Cinder Crypt.* His tune calls every lost child and every rat in the crypt. He was never paid, and has never forgiven it. **Planned fight:** his tune drags you sideways; rat waves skitter along the floor; pop his floating notes to break the tune, which opens his damage window.
5. **The Drowned Bride** — *The Drowned Mere.* Left at the altar and left in the lake. She rises with the mist, still in her veil, still waiting. **Planned fight:** water rises and falls in waves, covering lower platforms and slowing you; she only surfaces as it recedes.
6. **The Scarecrow King** — *Rotten Orchard.* He guards a harvest of nothing. The crows are his court, and they remember every face. **Planned fight:** he is fixed on his pole; crow flocks sweep the screen in telegraphed lanes; trap the crows to drop his shield, then hit him.
7. **The Bridge Troll** — *Bone Bridge.* Three tolls to cross: a coin, a secret, and a name. He accepts no substitutes, and what he does with the names is not discussed. **Planned fight:** each stomp sends a shockwave to jump over and crumbles bridge sections into gaps; he only takes damage while hunched after a stomp.
8. **The Apple Queen** — *The Poisoned Well.* One bite and the story ends. She is endlessly generous with her fruit. **Planned fight:** poisoned apples look like treasure and hurt if collected; apples left on the field heal her, so they must be popped.
9. **Old Bluebeard** — *Mirror Graveyard.* A courteous host with one rule: never open the last door. The graveyard is full of people who were curious. **Planned fight:** three doors; one opens each cycle to release a hazard; he is only hittable when he steps out, signaled by a glow.
10. **The Mirror Witch** — *The Glass Coffin.* The fairest of them all, and the last. She shows each hero the face they fear, and the glass never lies. **Planned fight:** three witches appear and only the real one takes damage; your mirrored decoy copies your moves.

## 9. Bubbles, chains and scoring

### 9.1 Your bubble
- Fired automatically, about **every 0.4 seconds**.
- Travels **about 100 pixels (roughly 4 tiles)**, slowing quickly, then **floats upward** as an empty bubble for about 6 seconds before popping harmlessly.
- Only a **moving** bubble traps a fiend or hurts a boss. A bubble that has stopped and started floating cannot trap anything.
- Radius is 9 pixels (modified by Big Bubbles and the Swollen Souls daily).
- **Twin Bubbles** (boon) fire a second, slower bubble 0.12 seconds behind the first.

### 9.2 Riding bubbles
Empty floating bubbles are **platforms from above**. Land on one while falling and you **ride it upward**. Jump while riding and the bubble **pops** under you. Touching a bubble from the side or below pops it. This lets you float up to the ceiling to reach trapped fiends stuck there.

### 9.3 Popping
- Touch a bubble holding a fiend: it bursts and the fiend becomes **treasure**.
- Touch an empty bubble: it just pops (a few points).
- Newly created bubbles are immune to your touch for a split second so you don't pop your own shots.

### 9.4 Chains (combo pops)
When any bubble pops, it triggers a **chain reaction**: every bubble (empty **or** containing a fiend) within **46 pixels** (about 2 tiles) pops about a tenth of a second later, and **each of those triggers their neighbors**, and so on. The pops ripple visibly across the screen.

- Every pop in a chain counts **1, 2, 3...** and that number is the **chain multiplier** for that pop.
- Treasure from a fiend pop is worth `treasure value × chain number`. The kill itself also scores `50 × chain number`.
- A chain of **3 or more** shows a big **"CHAIN xN!"** banner, shakes the screen and plays a rising growl. Fiend pops at chain 3+ drop **2 pieces of treasure** and at **5+ drop 3**.
- A chain of **3+ in a level** (any combination of fiends and empty bubbles) is what **opens the Hoard** (section 12).
- The strategy: **herd** several fiends close together, trap them in a cluster, then pop the lowest one and let the dominoes fall.

Chain radius is boosted by the hero/boons **Lantern**, **Wide Pop**, and the **Swollen Souls** daily modifier.

### 9.5 Treasure
Popped fiends drop treasure that bounces and waits **10 seconds**, blinking in its last two seconds. Treasure type and value depend on the realm:
- Treasure tiers (base points): **100, 200, 300, 500, 800, 1,200**.
- The tier a pop drops is `min(4, floor(realm ÷ 2))`, plus a **35% chance of one tier higher**. So early realms drop mostly cheap treasure and deep realms mostly premium treasure.
- Final value = tier value × chain number × your score multipliers.

### 9.6 Score multipliers
All points are multiplied by `Golden Touch (×1.25 per copy) × daily modifier (e.g. ×3 on Glass Heart)`.

## 10. Time pressure: the Dark Comes

Normal levels have a clock that keeps them to **30 to 60 seconds**:

| Level fiends | "The Dark Comes" at | The Hunger spawns at |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 26 s | 40 s |
| 3 | 29 s | 43 s |
| 4 | 32 s | 46 s |
| 5 | 35 s | 49 s |
| 6 | 38 s | 52 s |
| 7 | 41 s | 55 s |
| 8 | 44 s | 58 s |
| 9 | 47 s | 61 s |

(Formula: warning at `20 + 3 × fiends` seconds; the ghost 14 seconds after that.)

1. **Green bar** (top right of the HUD) counts down to the warning. It turns red and flashes once time is up.
2. **"THE DARK COMES!"**: every remaining fiend turns **angry** (red, 1.6× faster).
3. **"IT HUNGERS..."**: 14 seconds later a **black spectre with red eyes** appears at the top left and **hunts you through walls**. It **cannot be trapped or killed**, and it speeds up (46 px/s, +2 px/s each second, up to 86 px/s). Touching it costs a life.

**Boss levels have no timer** (no Dark Comes, no spectre) and the Hoard has its own 14-second countdown.

## 11. Stars, coins and rewards

### Stars (per level, 1–3)
| Star | Condition |
|---|---|
| ★ | Clear the level |
| ★ | Clear it **without being hit** |
| ★ | Clear it **before "The Dark Comes"** |

On boss levels there's no timer, so the third star simply requires the no-damage run too (the speed star is granted automatically). In Story mode your best stars per level are saved. In other modes stars are shown but only matter for the coin bonus.

### Coins (the meta currency)
- **Level clear:** `3 + 2 × stars` coins (so 5 to 9), **+15** for a boss.
- **Hoard gems:** **1 coin** per gem you collect.
- **Daily Curse finished:** **+40** coins.
- Coins are **saved across all modes** and spent on **heroes** (section 13). Coins are never required to progress.

### Score
Your score is shown in the HUD and on the end screen. It is a personal-bragging number for the Tales and Cursed Run, and the leaderboard value for the Daily Curse (best of the day).

## 12. The Hoard (secret rooms)

A **secret door** is how the game rewards skill and curiosity.

**How to open it:** clear a level where you achieved a **chain of 3 or more pops** — **or** defeat any **boss** (always guaranteed). When the last fiend falls, a glowing gold **"?" bubble** appears mid-screen with the text **"A DOOR..."**.

**How to enter:** touch it. You have about **7 seconds** after the clear (the game waits for you). Miss it and the game moves on.

**Inside the Hoard:**
- A golden room with a zig-zag of four platforms.
- **14 seconds** on the clock (shown in the HUD).
- **Five large gems** (1,500 points each) are placed on the platforms, and **a gem rain** (500 points each, one about every 0.4 seconds) falls from the ceiling for the whole time.
- **Every gem you touch is +1 coin** as well as points.
- No enemies. It's pure, joyful scrambling.
- At the end you proceed to the next level.

A strong run can collect roughly **30-40 gems**, so the Hoard is also the fastest way to earn coins for new heroes.

| 💥 **Wide Pop** | +30% chain radius |
| 🍯 **Sticky Bubbles** | Trapped fiends stay sealed **+2 seconds** |
| 🦘 **Spring Legs** | +6% jump height |
| 👟 **Quick Feet** | +12% move speed |
| 💖 **Extra Heart** | **+1 life** immediately |
| 🧲 **Magpie's Pull** | Treasure within about 64 px flies to you |
| 🪙 **Golden Touch** | +25% score |

## 15. Daily Curse modifiers

## 16. How levels are generated

Levels are **procedurally generated from a seed**, which is why the game can offer 1,000 fixed levels *and* infinite ones: the same seed + the same level number always produces the same layout.

**The grid:** 14 columns × 22 rows of 24-pixel tiles (the play area is 336 × 528 px; the whole screen with the HUD is 336 × 572). Columns 0 and 13 are solid walls.

**Floor and ceiling:** each level has a solid row at the bottom and top. From level 4 on, the pair may have a **gap** in the middle (none, a 2-tile gap or a 4-tile gap). The gap is identical top and bottom, which creates the **wrap-around**: fall through the bottom gap, reappear through the top gap.

**Platforms:** the generator picks one of **four row layouts** (platform heights): `[17,13,9,5]`, `[18,15,12,9,6,3]`, `[18,14,10,6]`, `[17,14,11,8,5]` (rows counted from the top). Then, for each row it picks one of **16 authored 12-tile patterns** (full bar, center gap, side ledges, stepped steps, checker-ish rows and so on), randomly mirrored.

**The reachability rule:** every platform segment must be within about 2 tiles of a platform tile in the row beneath it. A jump covers about **4.3 tiles of height**, so every level is guaranteed climbable from the floor. Rows that fail the check are re-rolled (up to 16 tries), with a full-width bar as the fallback.

**Fiend placement:** fiends are placed on random platform tops, kept apart from each other, and the floor's left side is kept clear for your spawn.

**Special layouts:** **Boss arenas** (section 8.2) and **the Hoard** are fixed layouts.

**Honest note:** the layouts are *generated from authored building blocks*, not individually hand-designed. The 1,000 Story levels are consistent and fair, but they share a vocabulary of 16 platform patterns. See section 22.

**Determinism:** Story and Tower use fixed seeds (the same for everyone). Cursed Run uses a random seed per run. Daily uses a hash of the date.

## 17. Difficulty curve

"Difficulty" equals the **level number** (Story), **floor number** (Tower, Cursed Run), or a fixed 14/20/26 (Daily).

| Difficulty | What changes |
|---|---|
| 1 | Two Grubblings. Introductory hints on levels 1–2 for new players. |
| 3 | **Toads** join |
| 8 | **Wolves** join |
| 14 | **Imps** (ranged attacks) join |
| 22 | **Wraiths** (flying chasers) join |
| 1 to 61 | Fiend count rises from 2 to the cap of 9 |
| 1 to 200 | Fiend speed rises by up to +80% |
| every 10th | A **boss**, with HP rising by about 1 per realm |

Time to clear also grows with fiend count, and so does the allowed time before The Dark Comes. By difficulty **61+** a typical level has 9 fiends of mixed types at up to 1.8× speed, which is a **hard** late-game that rewards chains and the Hoard.

## 18. Art, audio and feel

- **Visuals:** drawn entirely in code in a chunky pixel style on a 336×572 logical canvas scaled to your screen (crisp, nearest-neighbor look). Heroes, fiends, bosses, treasure, bubbles, particles and the backgrounds are all procedural.
- **Bubbles:** pale ghost-light, translucent, with a highlight. Bubbles with a fiend inside blink **red** as the seal weakens.
- **Screen feedback:** red flash on damage, screen shake on big chains and boss kills, particle bursts on every pop.
- **Audio:** all sound is **synthesized live** with the Web Audio API: jump, shot, trap, pop (pitch rises with chain length), growls, boss hits, clear jingle, treasure pickups. There are **no audio files**. Use the speaker button on the main menu to mute (saved).
- **Haptics:** short vibration on pops, bigger on damage (on devices that support it).
- **UI theme:** dark crimson and bone title, cyan and magenta buttons on near-black.

## 19. Save data

Progress is saved automatically in the browser's local storage on the device (key: `blooplands_save_v1`, the original working title, kept so early testers' saves still load). It stores:

- Coins, owned heroes, selected hero
- Best stars for every Story level and the furthest unlocked level
- Deepest Tower floor and deepest Cursed Run floor
- Daily best score, last-played date and streak
- Sound on/off

**Notes:** saves live **per device and per browser**. Clearing the browser's site data erases them. There are no accounts and no cloud sync in 1.0. Private/incognito windows may not save.

## 20. Technical reference and tuning guide

**Specs**
- One file (~54 KB). Plain HTML5 Canvas 2D, vanilla JavaScript, no dependencies, no network use.
- Logical resolution 336 × 572; scales to fit any screen and re-fits on rotation or resize. Backing-store resolution scales with device pixel ratio (up to 4×).
- Fixed 60 Hz simulation step (decoupled from display refresh), up to 5 catch-up steps per frame, so behavior is consistent on 60/90/120 Hz screens.
- Designed portrait-first; landscape displays a letterboxed portrait game.

**Core constants** (all near the top of the script; search for the names):

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `T` | 24 | tile size in px |
| `GR` | 900 | gravity (px/s²) |
| `JV` | 430 | jump velocity (≈4.3 tiles of height) |
| `SPD` | 105 | hero move speed (px/s) |
| `MAXF` | 420 | terminal fall speed |
| bubble cooldown | 0.4 s × `cd` stat | in `shoot()` |
| bubble launch | 300 px/s, decays ×e^(-3t) | in `shoot()` / `updateWorld()` |
| auto-fire range | 4.7 tiles × `range` | in `updPlayer()` |
| chain radius | 46 px × `chainR` | in `spread()` |
| chain delay | 0.1 s per link | in `spread()` |
| trap time | 6 s + stat | in `trap()` |
| Dark Comes | `20 + 3 × fiends` s | in `startLevel()` |
| Hunger delay | warning + 14 s | in `startLevel()` |
| treasure lifetime | 10 s | in `dropFruit()` |
| respawn invulnerability | 2.2 s | in `hurt()` |
| Hoard length | 14 s, rain every 0.38 s | in `enterSecret()` / `update()` |

**Easy balance levers** (if testers say the game is too hard or easy)
- *Too hard early:* lower the fiend-count formula (`2 + floor(√diff × 0.9)`), or raise `trapT`, or lengthen the Dark Comes time.
- *Bubbles feel weak:* raise the 300 launch speed or the 4.7-tile auto-fire range.
- *Bosses too long or short:* edit `Math.min(8+Math.floor(realm*1.2),30)`.
- *Coins too slow or fast:* edit `3+stars*2` and the heroes' `cost` values in the `CH` list.
- *Not enough chains:* raise the chain radius (46) or lower the secret-door threshold (`G.maxChain>=3`).

**Game state phases** (for anyone reading the code): `ready` → `play` → `clear` → (`secret`) → `finishing` (or the next level), with `dead` → `over` on game over.

**Test hook:** the page exposes `window.grimmpop` for automated testing; harmless in play.

## 21. Honest status: tested, untested, known limits

**What was verified**
- The script passes a syntax check.
- A headless simulation drove an automated bot through **all four modes** and through boss levels (levels 10, 25, 500; Tower floor 25), covering the full phase cycle, with **no runtime errors**.
- Targeted tests confirmed: bubbles **trap** fiends, trapped fiends **chain-pop**, the **Hoard** can be entered and completed (and pays coins), and wrap-around works.
- The look has had two passes (bright, then dark fairytale) with identical gameplay logic, and the game was renamed to Grimmpop with a re-run of the same tests.

**What has NOT been verified yet** (please test these on real phones)
- **Feel on a real touchscreen:** thumb-drag sensitivity, flick-to-jump reliability, and whether auto-fire aims the way players expect.
- **Balance:** the 30–60 second target length, boss fight length, and the difficulty ramp are by-the-numbers design, not playtest results. (The test bot is crude and dies often, so it says nothing about human difficulty.)
- **Audio on iOS/Safari** (sound starts after the first tap, as browsers require) and haptics support vary by device.
- **Pixel-level visuals** were not screenshot-reviewed in a real browser.

**Known limitations in 1.0**
- All ten bosses share one attack pattern (section 8.1).
- Levels come from 16 patterns, so a long session will start to feel familiar.
- No leaderboards or cloud saves; "same tower for everyone" is shared layout, not shared rankings.
- In Story mode, lives reset each level attempt (by design, to keep it friendly), so there's no persistent lives pressure.
- You can accidentally pop your own floating bubbles by walking into them (classic behavior, but it can cost a chain).
- No tutorial beyond brief on-screen hints on Story levels 1–2 for brand-new saves.
- Fiends can overlap each other and don't collide.
